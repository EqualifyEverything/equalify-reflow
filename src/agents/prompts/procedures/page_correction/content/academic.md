# Academic Content

This page contains academic paper content with formal conventions. Apply these additional formatting rules on top of the base procedure.

## Formatting conventions

### Italic (`*text*`)

Academic papers use italic for:
- **Latin phrases**: *et al.*, *in vivo*, *in vitro*, *a priori*, *a posteriori*, *ad hoc*, *i.e.*, *e.g.*, *cf.*, *viz.*, *inter alia*
- **Variables and mathematical symbols** in prose: "where *n* is the number of samples"
- **Journal and book titles** in reference lists and in-text mentions
- **Gene names, species names**: *E. coli*, *Drosophila*, *BRCA1*
- **Key terms** when first introduced or defined

In small fonts, italic can be subtle (slight rightward slant, thinner). If you cannot confidently distinguish italic from regular weight, note this in your reasoning but do not add formatting you are unsure about.

### Bold (`**text**`)

Bold is used sparingly in academic papers:
- **Theorem/definition/proposition labels**: "**Theorem 3.1.**" or "**Definition 2.**"
- **Key results or claims** in some paper styles

### Monospace (`` `text` ``)

Monospace/code formatting for:
- **Software and tool names**: `Python`, `TensorFlow`, `scikit-learn`
- **Function and method names**: `fit()`, `predict()`, `main()`
- **File paths and URLs**: `/usr/local/bin`
- **Code snippets inline**: "set `n_clusters=5`"
- **Command-line flags**: `--verbose`, `-O2`

In the image, monospace appears in a fixed-width font (Courier-like) distinct from the proportional serif body text.

## Citations

Academic citations appear in several styles:

- **Numbered**: `[1]`, `[2, 3]`, `[1-5]`
- **Author-year parenthetical**: `(Smith, 2024)`, `(Smith et al., 2024)`
- **Author-year narrative**: `Smith (2024) showed...`
- **Numbered superscript**: Similar to footnotes but referencing the bibliography

Common Docling errors with citations:
- Missing brackets: `1` instead of `[1]`
- Merged citations: `[1,2,3]` when the image shows `[1, 2, 3]` with spaces
- Split citations: a citation spanning a line break extracted as two bracket groups

## First-page elements

The first page often contains:
- Paper title (verify text accuracy)
- Author names and affiliations (check for superscript affiliation markers)
- Conference/journal header or footer (DOI, copyright notice)

## References section

If the page contains references:
- Reference entries are dense and error-prone -- check author names, years, journal titles (italic), volume numbers.
- Numbered references should maintain their numbering.
- URLs and DOIs are common -- verify accuracy.
